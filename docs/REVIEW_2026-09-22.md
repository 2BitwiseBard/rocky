# Project review — 2026-09-22

A full pass over the project before the reprint and the first hardware
order: CAD and mechanism, electronics and servos, software and the public
repo. Three review threads ran in parallel against the tree as of the D046
checkpoint (commit `27faeb9`); their findings are consolidated here and the
mechanical ones were acted on the same day (D047). Verdicts are dated; the
numbers come from the tree, the STEP model and the test runs, not from
memory.

## 1. Where the project actually stands

- **Sim, control, harness: real and green.** Driver 58/58, harness 21 fast + 1
  slow, gait 6/6, intent 14 — 85 passing on this laptop in 2.3 s. The bare
  wave gait walks 264 mm with 1° of tilt; the shipped self-righting policy
  reproduces at 10/20 (hybrid: RL rights the body, an analytic ramp stands it
  up; pure-RL success is 0/20 for every checkpoint). The negative retrains
  are recorded as negatives. This part of the project is portfolio-ready
  once the stale "12/20" strings and the URDF drift (fixed today) are gone.
- **CAD: disciplined, but every leg part was drawn around a servo that does
  not exist.** `servo_st3215.py` was a guessed box: 32 mm tall (real: 28.8
  flat-to-flat with 1.5 mm rims), horn top at 38 (real: 33.1), output axis
  11.25 mm from the end (real: 10.2), horn holes on 0/90/180/270 (real: a
  45° pattern), no rear idler, no cable connectors. D046 fixed the *class*
  of failure the first dry-fit hit (no assembly order, nothing captured),
  but it fixed it against the wrong servo, and its yaw stage reacted the
  leg's overturning moment through a 683ZZ bearing 15 mm from the horn
  (computed ~240 N on the bearing in a recovery push, PETG cap arm at
  130 MPa). None of the printed leg parts in the photo could ever have
  fit a real servo. **Nothing from the old leg should be reprinted.**
- **Electronics: the plan is coherent on paper and unbought.** All-ST3215
  is the right call for v1; the STS3250 knee is not justified by the torque
  audit, is unmodelled, and now lists at ~$88. The in-leg power path (three
  servos daisy-chained through 2 A connector pins) and the absence of a
  hardware low-voltage alarm are the two real gaps.
- **Repo: the content is strong, the shape was a "drop" archive.** Nested
  `rocky_v0.8.0_code/rocky/`, `patches/`, session prompt drafts, no LICENSE,
  no CI, a tracked `.mcp.json` with home-directory paths, three files that
  hard-coded the hip height. (All fixed the same evening: the tree is flat,
  MIT + CI + pyproject are in, see §6 item 7.)

## 3. Mechanical review (agent findings, with dispositions)

1. **Servo envelope wrong in five ways** → fixed (servo v2 from the STEP).
2. **Yaw stage structurally marginal-to-failing** (15 mm couple, bearing
   clamped solid by the axle head, 0.9 mm boss walls) → deleted; C-fork.
3. **No connector/idler relief on any face touching the servo bottom** →
   the cup leaves the whole cable side open; plug keep-out asserted.
4. **Retention lips clash with the real corner bosses / rims** → lips gone.
5. **Strap tap bores had 0.25 mm of wall** → straps gone.
6. **Tube socket Ø10.3 will print ~10.0; 683 pocket would split its land** →
   the bearing is gone; the tube socket and every other fit still wait on
   the fit-ladder numbers (see §6, item 1).
7. **Fit ladder printed, nothing consumes it** → still true; it is the
   first item of the print plan and `params.print` is where it lands.
8. **`deck_t` 4.0 in params vs 6.0 hard-coded in `iface.py`** → open (B27).
9. **Hand-as-foot is the most complex, least-tested subsystem** → a plain
   TPU foot on the I2 stub first (B25); the hand stays a later tool.
10. **Fixtures (jig, stand) weigh more than the legs** → print them last.

## 4. Electronics review (agent findings, with dispositions)

- **Servos: all-ST3215, 16 units** (15 + spare). One STS3250 only as a
  measured sample if the knee ever needs it. Order sheet v2 typos: the
  11-unit line is $252.89 not $253.89; six SCS0009 cost $54 at the single
  price, not $48.
- **Bus master: the Waveshare Servo Driver with ESP32 ($15.99) becomes
  core**, not optional — native UART timing, a hardware watchdog, and it
  is the cheapest way to make a bench servo move (web UI, no code).
- **In-leg power: fan 12 V out at the coxa** (XT30 to three short leads)
  instead of daisy-chaining 5–7 A through 2 A connector pins; set
  `PROTECT_CURRENT` ≈ 2 A per servo and archive the registers on bench day.
- **Pi 5 v rail**: separate 5 V/5 A buck with bulk caps, into the GPIO header
  (a bare buck on USB-C never negotiates PD and the Pi throttles USB).
- **LiPo safety**: balance-lead voltage alarm, XT60 loop key or a DC-rated
  switch, charge bag. None was on a sheet.
- **IMU**: BNO085 over I²C on a Pi clock-stretches; use UART-RVC or SPI.
  Foot switches to five GPIOs, not a wired-OR (the odometry EKF needs
  per-leg contact).
- **Torque audit**: hand mass double-counted (~7 % pessimistic), stall
  taken at 12 V not 10.5 V; verdict unchanged — ST3215 closes every case,
  the only warm number is a few seconds of recovery push.
- **Budget reality**: the $1,500 envelope is spent on paper once the
  batch-3 core items and Seeed prices are in; ~$1,250–1,450 on the
  all-ST3215 path. The bench milestone alone is ~$300–370.

## 6. Plan from here

1. **Fit ladder → params** (30 min of calipers). The measured dowel bore,
   M3 tap, heat-set pocket, tube socket and 683 pocket values go into
   `params.print`; regenerate. Until this is done every fit is a guess.
2. **Order the bench kit** (`bom/SHOPPING_LIST_2026-09-22.md`, part A):
   4 servos, the ESP32 driver, cables, screws, a bench supply. The servos
   are the gate for everything mechanical.
3. **Print the four coupons + one blank** (`docs/PRINT_PLAN_2026-09-22.md`,
   batch 1: ~2 h of printer time). Fit them. File the results.
4. **First servo arrives**: one M3 and one M2 screw into the horn; calipers
   on the case, rims, plateau, idler, plugs; the numbers go into
   `params.servo_st3215`; regenerate; reprint whatever moved (probably
   only the coupler and the yaw hub, by design).
5. **Print one leg** (batch 2) and the bench jig; drive it from the ESP32
   board's web page the day the servos land, then from `bench/` scripts.
6. **Body** (batch 3): deck + five bases; the shell waits.
7. **Repo pass** (done 2026-09-22 evening): tree flattened to the code root,
   MIT LICENSE, GitHub Actions CI on the fast suites + parity, `pyproject`
   with real extras, demo GIFs and a dated STATUS block in one README.
